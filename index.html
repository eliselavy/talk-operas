Tool shapes thought — the choice of tool is never neutral; using AWS versus GRid500, or Omeka versus a custom stack, actively influences research design and what questions become askable Versatility as a core skill — knowing when to tinker (build, adapt, hack) versus when to trust an existing tool is itself a research competency, not just a technical one Control as an open science value — maintaining the ability to inspect, modify, and share your toolchain is part of reproducibility; black-box off-the-shelf solutions can undermine this

GRid500 High-performance computing cluster Data sovereignty — GRid500 keeps data on institutional/national infrastructure, making GDPR compliance and data governance straightforward; AWS implies data leaving to commercial US-based servers, raising legal and ethical questions for sensitive research data Cost model — GRid500 is typically funded institutionally (no per-use billing); AWS follows a pay-as-you-go model that can escalate quickly at scale Reproducibility — both support reproducible pipelines, but GRid500 environments are often fixed and stable; AWS offers more flexibility but also more configuration drift risk Open access alignment — GRid500 aligns naturally with public research mandates and open science values; AWS is commercial infrastructure, though widely used in open science workflows
